***Abstract***

**Edge-cloud collaborative systems increasingly serve AI inference on fragmented, heterogeneous edge servers. Decentralized request handlers such as EPARA's offload requests to peers in proportion to their idle capacity, but treat every server that hosts a model as a lawful destination. We formulate trust-constrained routing as goodput maximization subject to zero violations, using multilevel-security labels (clearance and trust domain), an explicit threat model in which ingress servers relay but do not process data, and a closed-form expression showing that the violation rate of trust-oblivious routing is fixed by the label distributions. We present TRACE, a decentralized router that combines a hard trust filter, SLO-aware admission and an adaptively enabled best-fit clearance preference with capacity-proportional offloading, at about 20 µs per decision. In simulation with 30 heterogeneous servers, 30 evaluation seeds and Holm-corrected paired tests, compliance costs 1.2 points of SLO attainment for a centralized dispatcher with perfect information and 2.7 points for decentralized routing at the default traffic mix, rising with the share of sensitive traffic. TRACE attains 0.960 compliant SLO attainment at 80% load, 2.9 points below the centralized oracle; it is tied with a trust-filtered join-the-shortest-queue router below saturation and ahead of it, of trust-filtered power-of-two-choices and round-robin routing and of static per-tier pools at and beyond saturation (by 8 to 34 points at 100% load). SLO-aware admission is the decisive element in overload, raising every router's attainment at 100% load by more than 50 points. Aligning data sensitivity with ingress clearance reduces, but does not remove, violations of trust-oblivious offloading (13.5% at full alignment). We also test whether weighting offload decisions by energy headroom helps on energy-constrained sites: it is harmful when energy is scarce and an empty buffer throttles work ({{a:B_energy|TF+EW - TF phi=0.6 outage=False}} points), gives small gains that are not significant after correction under hard outages, and is neutral when supply is sufficient. All parameters are documented assumptions, and the simulator and raw results are public.**
